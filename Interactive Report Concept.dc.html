Five rules we designed to

01

Friendlier, never lighter

Plain language and clearer structure. Every number and quotation stays exactly as published.

02

Two depths, one page

A seven-minute read and a twenty-four-minute read live in the same document. The reader picks.

03

Accessible by construction

Native semantics, keyboard-first, AA contrast — not a toolbar bolted on at the end.

04

People before findings

The research is qualitative. Voices get their own section, filterable by who is speaking.

05

Blocks, not bespoke

Everything is a reusable block type, so the next publication takes days, not months.

06

No interaction without a reason

A filter earns its place only when it answers a question a reader actually has.

The central interaction

Plain summary, or full text

Plain summary · 7 min

Every chapter keeps its pull-quote lede — one paragraph, written for a reader with no background in digital rights.

Numbers, visuals, timeline, voices and recommendations all stay. Only the long-form academic passages collapse.

Full text · 24 min

The reported argument, in the report’s own voice, with its citations and caveats intact.

Default state, so researchers and journalists land on the substance without hunting for it.

Alongside it: a text-size control that scales the whole reading column, a contents drawer, and a progress bar in the header — the three things people reach for in a long document.

Case in point

The fold-out chart, made clickable

A wall-sized chart is the best thing in the printed report and the worst thing to read on a phone. Same information, four affordances added.

Read at a size that works

The grid scrolls sideways instead of shrinking type to 6pt.

Open a cell for the detail

Wording and analysis appear above the grid, instead of in eight-point marginalia.

Highlight the undefined words

The print version’s yellow marker becomes a switch — and an argument you can see all at once.

Say it in words too

Colour never carries meaning alone; every cell states which way it cuts.

Answered

Footnotes and glossary, three ways at once

Layer one

A real anchor

Every marker is a link to a numbered endnote, and every endnote links back to where you were. Works with no scripting at all.

Layer two

A tooltip on top

Shown on hover and on keyboard focus, announced to screen readers, hoverable so links inside stay reachable, dismissed with Escape.

Layer three

Written out in full

A Notes section and a Glossary at the end of every report — so the content is never trapped inside an interaction.

Glossary terms sit inline on first use and as a "key terms" strip near the top, so a reader with no background in digital rights has a way in before the argument starts.

Non-negotiable

A report about exclusion cannot exclude its readers

Native semantics

Real headings, lists, figures and <details> — so screen readers and keyboards work without custom scripting.

Visible focus, skip link

A 3px focus ring on everything interactive, and a skip link straight to the report body.

Reader-set text size

Scales the reading column up to 150% without breaking layout, on top of browser zoom.

AA contrast throughout

Ink on warm paper, one accent used only where it passes at the size it is used.

Reduced motion respected

Smooth scrolling and transitions switch off when the reader’s system asks them to.

Described figures

Every chart is also stated in words and numbers; the map carries a full text description.

Six decisions before we template this

Nepali alongside English?

A language switch changes type choices, line lengths and the plain-language pass.

Which citations make the cut

The pattern is settled; the editorial call is not. Each report currently carries 10–11 notes on the load-bearing claims, against 100+ in the PDF. All of them, or the ones that matter?
