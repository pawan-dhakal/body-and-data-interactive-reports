Thematic Analysis

Four Structural Fractures Across Nepal’s Digital Landscape

Across biometric registration, criminal law enforcement, and disability access, Body & Data’s research reveals four recurring structural patterns of digital marginalization:

Fracture 01

Consent Is Missing by Design

From mandatory biometric enrollment for banking and social security to reproductive privacy exceptions in the Civil Code, individual bodily and digital consent is systematically bypassed in favor of state convenience and patriarchal control.

Fracture 02

Undefined & Moralistic Terms

Statutes routinely rely on vague phrasing—"public morality", "decency", "social harmony", "character" (charitra)—that grant arbitrary discretionary power to police and courts while silencing marginalized and queer voices.

Fracture 03

Protection Operates as Control

Protective frameworks frequently morph into surveillance tools. Women and persons with disabilities find their digital autonomy restricted under the guise of family safety, while the state uses security mandates to monitor citizen movement.

Fracture 04

Nobody Was Consulted

Digital public infrastructure and cyber policies are formulated through centralized, top-down processes that bypass local ward offices, disability organizations, feminist researchers, and affected citizens on the ground.
