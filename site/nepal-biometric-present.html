Nepal’s Biometric Present

Governance, Accessibility and Accountability

In November 2018, the Government of Nepal introduced the National Identity Card (NID) with the ambition of enrolling all citizens biometrically by 2023. This study investigates the social consequences, bureaucratic friction, and constitutional challenges of Nepal’s digital ID program across administrative centers and remote rural settlements.

Headline Finding

"The National ID in Nepal has developed primarily as an instrument of state surveillance and bureaucratic gatekeeping, rather than an enabler of welfare delivery. It reinforces historical inequalities around citizenship transmission while introducing biological and infrastructural hurdles for rural manual laborers, elderly citizens, and persons with disabilities."

Acronyms & Key Terminologies

Definitions and institutional abbreviations utilized across this study, drawn from global privacy frameworks (GDPR), national statutes, and field observations.

DONIDCR

Department of National Identity and Civil Registration (Ministry of Home Affairs).

NIDMIS

National Identity Management Information System.

DPI

Digital Public Infrastructure (Foundational identity, payments, and data sharing systems).

UIDAI / Aadhaar

Unique Identification Authority of India 12-digit biometric identity system.

Biometric Data

Personal data capturing an individual's physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics (fingerprints, facial geometry, iris scans) through technological processing to confirm unique identity. When collected without strong legal protections, it enables extra-judicial surveillance as individuals' bodies become quantifiable.

Datafication

The process of transforming human activities, social interactions, and physical traits into quantifiable digital data stored and analyzed by public and private entities.

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)

A set of foundational digital systems—primarily digital identification, fast payments, and data exchange—serving as intermediate building blocks for state service delivery.

Profiling

Any form of automated processing of personal data to evaluate, analyze, or predict aspects of an individual's behavior, financial status, health, location, or reliability.

Executive Summary

Executive Summary & Core Research Questions

"In November 2018, the Government of Nepal introduced the National ID to provide digital identity to its citizens. The government ambitiously claimed to enroll all residents biometrically by 2023. Each enrolled person would receive a unique 10-digit identification number... This study examines the social consequences and on-the-ground effects of the National ID."

Question 01

Why is the Nepali state pursuing digital identification projects with such urgency?

Question 02

How is the government executing its digital identification scheme on the ground across districts?

Question 03

How are citizens, local governments, and civil society engaging with this ambitious biometric initiative?

As NID cards are not yet widely used in digital form—mainly because public offices lack card-reading infrastructure—this study presents an overview of the project’s design, planning, and implementation, with a focus on the enrollment process. It builds on Body & Data’s earlier research conducted during the pilot phase of the National ID in 2023.

Our investigation reveals that while the government presents the National ID as a technical solution for administrative modernization and welfare distribution, in practice it operates within a centralized security framework under the Ministry of Home Affairs. The distribution of cards has lagged severely, creating widespread dependency on paper printouts of National Identity Numbers (NIN), while technical errors in data capture disproportionately penalize manual laborers, elderly women, and marginalized castes.

Chapter I

I. Introduction & The Concept of National ID

"The National ID program is promoted by state officials as a transformative 'game changer' for Nepal. However, by conditioning digital identity strictly on possession of citizenship certificates, it embeds historical exclusionary structures into digital public infrastructure."

The concept of a national digital identity gained constitutional footing in Nepal following the promulgation of the Constitution in 2015. Article 15 provides that every citizen must have a clear identity, while Article 51(f)(7) mandates an "integrated national identity management information system" to coordinate citizen data across state services and development plans19.

However, digital ID systems do not operate in a vacuum. In Nepal, historical controversies surrounding citizenship laws have left an estimated 5 million residents without citizenship certificates32. By making citizenship cards mandatory for NID enrollment, the state risks permanently locking millions of residents out of public services, social security allowances, banking, and mobile SIM registration.

Furthermore, gender discriminatory clauses in citizenship transmission continue to prevent single mothers from passing citizenship to their children without intrusive administrative hurdles. LGBTQI+ individuals face arbitrary refusals when attempting to register their gender identity accurately, as NID enrollment software enforces rigid binary categories (Male/Female).

Chapter II

II. The Birth of National Identity Card

"The NID project originated in 2009 with Asian Development Bank financing. It has been repeatedly contested in Parliament over single-source procurement awards to French defense contractor Morpho Safran (Idemia) and its shift into a new security space."

The concept of a national digital identity was first introduced in government policy in July 2009 as part of a vision for e-Governance. In 2011, the government established the National ID Management Centre (NIDMC). With support from the Asian Development Bank (ADB), a USD 14 million pilot was launched in 2012 covering 110,000 citizens in Panchthar District and 7,000 civil servants in Singha Durbar17.

In 2016, the government awarded the pilot contract to French multinational Morpho Safran (renamed Idemia in 2017). The contract was awarded solely on technical qualification, bypassing competitive public bidding thresholds. Opposition MPs raised serious objections in Parliament, citing violations of the Public Procurement Act22.

In 2021, Idemia received a follow-on contract to supply an additional 12 million NID cards—again without competitive bidding. Senior government officials noted that partnering with a foreign private vendor for the entire biometric value chain creates severe institutional dependencies and unvetted privacy vulnerabilities37.

Stated Goals vs. Documented Realities

Promise 01: Administrative Modernization & Efficiency Expand ▾

State Promise

Centralized identity management reduces bureaucratic redundancy, streamlines inter-departmental workflows, and ends duplicate records across ministries.

Field Reality

Local wards lack card readers and stable power. Citizens must carry paper NIN slips alongside physical citizenship cards, effectively creating an additional layer of red tape.

Promise 02: Universal Digital Financial Inclusion Expand ▾

State Promise

Mandatory NID linking for bank accounts and SIM cards accelerates financial inclusion and prevents financial fraud across the banking sector.

Field Reality

Rural citizens lacking NID slips are denied basic savings accounts and SIM cards, cutting off informal workers and women from remittances.

Promise 03: Seamless Welfare & Social Security Delivery Expand ▾

State Promise

Biometric de-duplication ensures elderly allowances and disability pensions reach rightful recipients without leakages.

Field Reality

Supreme Court had to intervene in July 2024 to suspend mandatory NID for welfare allowances after elderly and disabled citizens were denied quarterly stipends.

Chapter III

III. How the Government is Working

"The NID rollout is executed through a top-down administrative structure. Local municipalities and ward offices bear the brunt of operational friction without being consulted on digital capacity, infrastructure deficits, or literacy barriers."

Across the country, local governments have been exposed to digital workflows primarily through financial software like SuTRA (Sub-National Treasury Regulatory Application). When tasked with biometric enrollment, ward officials faced severe hardware shortages, daily server crashes, and frequent power outages.

Because official pre-registration portals are inaccessible to non-English speakers and require continuous broadband connectivity, citizens have been forced to rely on private cyber cafés and local brokers charging unregulated fees between Rs 200 and Rs 500 per form.

The 6-Step Enrolment Friction Journey

Detailed documented failure points encountered by citizens at each stage of the registration pipeline.

1
Step 1: Document Gathering & Verification Citizenship card, migration papers, marriage registration

Applicants must present physical original citizenship certificates. Married women whose husbands' names are not inscribed on their citizenship card must obtain marriage registration certificates, requiring multiple trips to ward offices and fee payments.

⚠️ Field failure point: High exclusion of single mothers and rural women whose marriages are unregistered.

2
Step 2: Online Pre-Registration Form Digital data entry via DONIDCR web portal

Due to English-language interfaces and server timeouts, citizens are pushed to commercial cyber cafés. Operators frequently misspell names, switch birthdates between Bikram Sambat and Gregorian calendars, and input incorrect permanent addresses.

⚠️ Field failure point: Generated 161,113 rejected cards due to operator typos.

3
Step 3: Biometric Capture Station 10 Fingerprints, 2 Iris Scans, Live Facial Photo

Optical fingerprint scanners fail to capture worn papillary ridges of agricultural laborers, construction workers, and the elderly. Iris scanners fail on citizens with cataracts or narrow eye apertures common in Janajati communities.

⚠️ Field failure point: Operators repeatedly force citizens to rub hands with oil or apply pressure, causing distress.

4
Step 4: Central Verification & NIN Issuance Server de-duplication and 10-digit number generation

Citizens receive a paper receipt with their 10-digit NIN via SMS. In areas with poor mobile reception, SMS delivery fails, leaving applicants without confirmation.

⚠️ Field failure point: "Pending Verification" status leaves thousands in administrative limbo for months.

5
Step 5: Smart Card Printing & Logistics Centralized card embossing at Singha Durbar

Only 1.65 million cards printed out of 16.35 million enrolled due to chip procurement bottlenecks and limited printing machine throughput.

⚠️ Field failure point: Over 90% of enrolled citizens operate indefinitely on paper slips.

6
Step 6: Ward Verification & Service Delivery Card reading and live biometric authentication

Public service counters lack card readers and API connectivity. Offline authentication is impossible, forcing staff back to manual paper ledger lookups.

⚠️ Field failure point: Complete breakdown of promised "interoperable digital governance".

Chapter IV

IV. Disputes, Differences & Exclusions

"Biometric systems operate on assumptions of standardized human bodies. In practice, age, manual labor, physical impairment, and gender diversity create systemic exclusion mechanisms that deny citizens their constitutional rights."

In both Sarlahi and Khotang, data collectors reported extensive difficulties capturing biometrics from elderly residents and manual laborers. When fingerprints fail to register, enrollment software rejects the record as 'incomplete'.

Citizens with physical disabilities face inaccessible enrolment centers located on upper floors without ramps or elevators. Furthermore, trans and non-binary individuals are forced into binary gender boxes that contradict their legal identities.

Exclusion 01

Citizenship Deprivation Trap

5M+ residents lacking citizenship are barred from NID, cutting off access to bank accounts, welfare pensions, and SIM cards.

Exclusion 02

Laborers' Worn Fingerprints

Agricultural and construction workers whose papillary ridges are worn down face repeated hardware rejection during enrollment.

Exclusion 03

Optical Iris Scanner Bias

Cataracts and narrow eye apertures cause frequent capture timeouts on elderly citizens and Janajati community members.

Exclusion 04

Physical Inaccessibility

Enrollment centers located in non-accessible government buildings without ramps, elevators, or sign language interpreters.

Exclusion 05

Rigid Binary Gender Markers

System forces trans and non-binary citizens into Male/Female markers, erasing third-gender constitutional protections.

Exclusion 06

The "Pending Verification" Trap

Applicants whose records show minor spelling discrepancies remain stuck in unverified status without recourse.

Chapter V

V. Right to Privacy & Biometric Dataflow

"Nepal currently lacks a comprehensive data protection law and an independent data privacy commission. Centralizing sensitive biometrics and personal records into a single honeypot creates systemic national security and personal privacy risks."

While the Individual Privacy Act 2018 guarantees the right to privacy under Article 28 of the Constitution, Section 5 of the National ID Act contains broad exceptions allowing state agencies and third-party private entities to access biometric data for vague 'public interest' purposes without judicial warrants.

Furthermore, recurring cyber attacks—including the severe DoS attack on government servers on 31 January 2023—demonstrate that Nepal's digital infrastructure remains acutely vulnerable to data breaches.

6-Hop Biometric Dataflow Tracer

Tracking the path of personal biometrics from citizen enrollment to central servers and private integrations.

Hop 01

Ward Enrolment Kit

Offline local capture of 10 prints, 2 irises, portrait photo, and demographic records onto contractor laptop.

Hop 02

Encrypted Upload

Batch transmission over public internet lines to the central NIDMIS server at the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Hop 03

Central Singha Durbar Data Centre

Permanent biometric storage and de-duplication matching via Idemia AFIS/ABIS algorithm engines.

Hop 04

Hetauda Disaster Recovery

Secondary mirrored copy maintained at the Government Integrated Data Centre in Hetauda.

Hop 05

Foreign Vendor Infrastructure

Software maintenance and cryptographic key access by French multinational Idemia technicians.

Hop 06

Onward Integrations

API connections to Passports, Social Security, Land Registry, Commercial Banks (KYC), and Telecom SIM databases.

Chapter VI

VI. Way Forward: 18 Actionable Recommendations

Policy recommendations segmented by key stakeholder groups for building a rights-respecting digital identity framework.

01 Halt Mandatory Linkages for Essential Public Services

Ensure that essential public services—including social security allowances, public healthcare, basic schooling, and emergency relief—are never conditioned upon possession of an NID card or biometric verification.

02 Enact Comprehensive Data Protection Legislation

Pass an omnibus Data Protection Act adhering to international human rights standards, enshrining purpose limitation, data minimization, and explicit consent.

03 Establish an Independent Data Protection Commission

Create an autonomous regulatory body with investigatory and enforcement powers to oversee biometric security and audit government and private data practices.

04 Implement Robust Manual Exception Handling

Mandate alternative verification mechanisms for elderly citizens, manual laborers with unreadable fingerprints, and persons with disabilities.

05 Reform Discriminatory Citizenship Laws

Overhaul citizenship transmission laws to ensure equal rights for Nepali mothers and dismantle structural exclusions that leave 5M+ residents undocumented.

06 Invest in Foundational Digital Infrastructure

Equip local ward offices with stable power backups, high-speed connectivity, and trained technical staff before rolling out mandatory digital platforms.

11 Adhere to Privacy by Design & Purpose Limitation

Private financial institutions and telecom providers must not store raw biometric packets or use NID data for commercial profiling and targeted advertising.

12 Maintain Non-Biometric Customer Verification

Commercial banks must offer accessible non-biometric KYC alternatives for customers with physical impairments or missing documentation.

13 Promote Transparent Algorithmic Audits

Technology vendors must publish independent security audits, algorithmic bias assessments, and vulnerability assessments of all supplied software.

14 Conduct Independent Biometric Harms Monitoring

Document and publicize instances of welfare denial, biometric failure, and discrimination experienced by marginalized groups across rural districts.

15 Strengthen Grassroots Digital Literacy

Organize community workshops demystifying digital public infrastructure, informed consent, and personal data protection in local indigenous languages.

16 Build Cross-Movement Coalitions

Unite feminist organizations, disability rights groups, indigenous federations, and privacy litigators to challenge surveillance creep.
